# Research Report

## Contextual Inquiry for Canvas UX

### Summary of Work

Conducted a full length Contextual Inquiry interview with 2 student volunteers that use Canvas for schoolwork. Each of the 2 sessions involved observing the participants as they navigated Canvas in real time and asking open-ended questions about how they track assignments, prioritize tasks, and manage workload. The sessions were focused on understanding existing behaviors, pain points, and decision-making processes. Raw, rough notes and observations were documented on Google Docs during each session.

### Motivation

Since the goal of our project is to design a Chrome extension that enhances Canvas with improved task management, workload analysis, and AI task recommendation, I believe that before we start coding specific features into the extension, we need to understand how students currently interact with Canvas and the issues that they face. Rather than relying on assumption, the interviews allow us to observe real behaviors in students, which ensures that the features we implement are useful for actual user workflows.

### Time Spent

I spent around 20 minutes creating meaningful interview questions and possible follow-up questions to come prepared. Each interview took around 30 minutes. Since I didn't analyze any of my notes yet, that is all the time I spent.

### Results

A full contextual inquiry with meaningful questions based on UX principles[^1]. Since no analysis has been done yet, I only have raw notes on participant answers for: how students determine what to work on, when to start, what’s due, students’ use of external tools, methods for estimating workload, stories on situations where assignments were missed or misjudged, features that students wish were in Canvas, etc. More details and observations will come next week after I do a complete analysis.
